Academic Advising

Guidance when you need it

Advisers help you plan your subjects, stay on track, and make confident decisions about your future.

How advising works

Academic advising ensures that every CICS student receives personalized guidance throughout their college journey. Students are assigned to designated faculty academic advisers (or may consult their program department chair) to assist with curriculum planning, subject sequencing, prerequisite verification, and monitoring academic progress.

When to seek advising: Students should consult their adviser before each enrollment period, when selecting elective or specialization tracks, when adding or dropping subjects, or whenever experiencing academic difficulties. Before meeting with your adviser, please prepare your student ID number, an updated curriculum checklist, latest grades, and a proposed list of courses.

During the session, your adviser evaluates your progress, reviews prerequisite eligibility, answers program-related questions, and approves your recommended course load. After the consultation, proceed with online enlistment and retain a copy of your advised subjects for your records.

  • Subject planning and course loading
  • Academic standing and retention support
  • Shifting, crediting, and program questions
  • Career and graduate study guidance

Enrollment & Registration Guidelines

Enroll in four steps

Tap each step as you finish it to track your progress. Check the Announcements section on the Home page for deadlines.

Review curriculum & standing

Log in to the Student Portal to check your curriculum checklist, completed subjects, remaining requirements, and prerequisites. Verify your academic standing, ensure prior semester grades are complete, and settle any office clearances before planning your schedule.

Consult your adviser

Meet with your academic adviser during the designated advising window to review your proposed courses. Confirm prerequisite compliance, discuss elective/specialization tracks, resolve academic concerns, and receive approval for your recommended course load.

Register subjects online

Access the DLSU-D Student Portal during your assigned enrollment schedule. Enlist in your approved subjects, lecture, and laboratory sections, ensuring you adhere to enrollment instructions and that there are no schedule conflicts.

Verify & finalize enrollment

Review your assessed fees and official Certificate of Registration (COR). Settle tuition and fees through authorized bank channels, online payment facilities, or the university Cashier, and verify your official enrolled status.

Internship / On-the-Job Training (OJT)

Learn in the real world

Gain professional experience with partner companies before you graduate.

OJT Requirements

Training Hours: BSIT requires 486 hours (approx. 12 weeks); BSCS requires a minimum of 240 hours during the practicum term.

Eligibility: Regular senior standing with all prerequisite computing courses completed and academic clearances secured.

Mandatory Official Forms:

  • Form C (EOMS-ITD-FRM-014): Formal OJT Endorsement Letter signed by the Practicum Coordinator endorsing the student to a prospective host company.
  • Form D (EOMS-ITD-FRM-015): Practicum Agreement Form (PAF) detailing company info, job description, supervisor, and work period in 3 copies.
  • Form E (EOMS-ITD-FRM-016): Notarized Waiver for Onsite OJT with student acknowledgment, parent/guardian consent, and attached copy of valid parent/guardian ID (3 copies).
  • Form F (EOMS-ITD-FRM-017): Daily Attendance Sheet for recording daily time-in/out and accumulated hours (or company timecard/DTR).

Partner Companies

Students must select a host company or organization that offers rigorous technical training aligned with their degree program and specialization track (Software Technology, Network Technology, Intelligent Systems, or Game Development).

Partnership / MOU: Host companies must have an active Memorandum of Understanding (MOU / Form A) signed by the CICS Dean and company representative. If no MOU exists, students must coordinate with the Practicum Coordinator to initiate one before starting training.

Placement Assistance: Consult the CICS Practicum Coordinator (Ms. Emelyn D. Mayuga) and the university placement office for active partner listings, industry contacts, and deployment advisories. Partner listings are updated each academic term.

OJT Process

A step-by-step roadmap from orientation to internship completion:

  1. Attend the mandatory Practicum Orientation and verify curriculum prerequisites.
  2. Request Form C (Endorsement Letter) and apply to prospective host companies with your resume.
  3. Upon acceptance, verify the host company MOU (Form A) with the Practicum Coordinator.
  4. Accomplish Form D (Practicum Agreement Form) with your company supervisor.
  5. Execute and notarize Form E (Waiver for Onsite OJT) with parent/guardian consent and valid ID.
  6. Submit all pre-internship documents to your OJT Teacher for clearance before day one.
  7. Render hours; record daily attendance on Form F and learning logs on Form G (Status Journal).
  8. Participate in the scheduled faculty company visitation (EOMS-ITD-FRM-028).
  9. Complete required hours and submit Form H (System Study), Form J (Evaluations), and the company Certificate of Completion.

Evaluation

Student performance during the practicum is evaluated across multiple criteria by the host company supervisor and the college faculty:

  • Supervisor Performance Evaluation (Form J / EOMS-ITD-FRM-021): The immediate job supervisor evaluates the student on 8 comprehensive criteria (100 points total), covering quality of work, technical competence, punctuality, attendance, initiative, and professional demeanor.
  • Faculty Supervision: The OJT Teacher verifies rendered training hours on Form F, checks daily journal entries (Form G), and assesses overall performance.
  • Student Deliverables: Students must submit a Project Report / System Study (Form H / EOMS-ITD-FRM-019), a Reflection Paper (Form J / EOMS-ITD-FRM-022), and a Student Evaluation of the Host Company (Form L / EOMS-ITD-FRM-024).
  • Completion Crediting: Final credit is awarded upon satisfying all required hours, achieving a passing supervisor rating, and submitting the official Certificate of Completion issued by the host company.

Laboratory Policies

Computer lab rules & booking

Help us keep the labs safe, clean, and available for everyone.

Lab rules

  • Present your student ID before entering
  • No food or drinks inside the laboratory
  • Do not install unauthorized software
  • Log out and shut down when finished
  • Report damaged equipment to the technician

How to book a lab

Students and recognized student organizations needing computer laboratory facilities for academic projects, research, or approved co-curricular activities must follow the standard booking procedure:

  1. Check available laboratories and verify software/hardware capabilities for your activity.
  2. Check schedule availability for your preferred date and time.
  3. Prepare booking details: activity purpose, course/organization, requested time slot, expected headcount, and supervising faculty member.
  4. Submit a formal lab reservation request to the laboratory staff at least 2 working days in advance.
  5. Await written or digital approval from the laboratory staff before occupying the room.
  6. Adhere strictly to lab rules: log in, keep workstations clean, report hardware defects, and power down after use.

Scholarships & Financial Assistance

Make your education affordable

Explore programs that can help with tuition and other costs.

Academic scholarships

DLSU-D awards merit-based scholarships to recognize and support outstanding academic performance:

  • Entrance Scholarships: Granted to incoming first-year students graduating with high honors (Valedictorian, Salutatorian) or top scores on the DLSU-D College Entrance Exam, offering partial to 100% tuition discounts.
  • Academic Honors / Dean's Honor List: Merit-based tuition discounts awarded each semester to continuing students achieving high scholastic standing (consistent GPA retention criteria with no failing or incomplete grades).
  • How to Apply: Administered through the Admissions and Scholarships Office (AdSO) at the Ayuntamiento de Gonzalez Building. Continuing scholarships are evaluated automatically per semester based on official registrar records. For inquiries, email scholarship@dlsud.edu.ph.
Government grants and programs

Eligible CICS students may access external and government-funded educational assistance programs:

  • CHED Scholarships & Tertiary Education Subsidy (TES): Tuition subsidies and stipends administered by the Commission on Higher Education for qualified Filipino students enrolled in recognized priority degree programs.
  • DOLE Special Program for Employment of Students (SPES): Youth employment program coordinated with the Department of Labor and Employment to support deserving students during academic breaks.
  • Requirements & Application Period: Applicants typically submit a Certificate of Enrollment, academic grades, and proof of family income (Certificate of Indigency or Income Tax Return). Schedules follow CHED and local government announcements; coordinate with AdSO for active application windows.
Student assistantship

The Student Assistantship Program (SAP) offers valuable work-study experience on campus while offsetting tuition fees:

  • Scope of Work: Student assistants render designated hours per week assisting in college offices, administrative departments, or CICS computer laboratories with technical, administrative, and laboratory tasks.
  • Benefits: Tuition discounts or hourly credit allowances applied directly toward school tuition and fees.
  • Eligibility: Open to bonafide undergraduate students in good academic standing with no disciplinary record. Work duty hours are structured around class schedules to avoid conflicts. Apply through the Student Welfare Office or AdSO.
Payment plans and financial aid

DLSU-D provides flexible tuition payment options and need-based institutional assistance:

  • Flexible Payment Plans: Tuition may be paid in full upon enrollment or arranged through installment schedules (initial enrollment down payment with remaining balances due prior to major exam periods).
  • Student Financial Aid Grants: Need-based institutional grants available to economically challenged yet deserving students, evaluated per academic year.
  • Payment Gateways: Settle fees via authorized bank partners, online payment channels, or the campus Cashier's Office. For detailed statement assessments or installment adjustments, consult the Accounting Office or check the Student Portal.
