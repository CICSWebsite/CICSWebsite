BS Computer Science (BSCS)

Bachelor of Science in Computer Science

Program Description

The BSCS program prepares students to be highly competent and certified in the areas of computing theory and applications. It trains them in abstract reasoning, analytical thinking, and research, with tracks in Robotics or Mobile and Game Development.

Specializations are also offered in Game Development and Intelligent Systems (see below).

Program Goals
Apply concepts of computing and mathematics in different domains of computer science.
Design, develop, test and evaluate various solutions using algorithms for complex computing problems that take full advantage of the usability of computer resources.
Document computer-based systems by writing effective reports for risk-assessment impact.
Communicate effectively with computing and non-computer science professionals about computing activities.
Recognize the legal, social, ethical, and professional issues involved in the utilization of computer technology and address these issues.
Engage in continuing professional development through performing independent or collaborative research as computing professionals.

BS Information Technology (BSIT)

Bachelor of Science in Information Technology

Program Description

The BSIT program focuses on the rigorous training of students with the latest IT concepts and applications. It provides a practical approach to studying the various facets and latest applications of the IT industry, with tracks in Network Technology or Software Technology.

Program Goals
Apply concepts of computing in different domains of information technology.
Identify and analyze the problem and define the computing requirements appropriate to its solution.
Design, develop, test and evaluate various solutions using modern computing tools for complex computing problems.
Document the computer-based systems by writing effective reports for end-users.
Work effectively as an individual or as a member of a team during software development for company-based systems.
Manage and maintain system security, reliability, and efficiency.
Communicate effectively with the computing community and non-IT professionals about computing activities.
Recognize the legal, social, ethical, and professional issues involved in the utilization of computer technology and address these issues.
Engage in continuing professional development through performing independent or collaborative research as computing professionals.
